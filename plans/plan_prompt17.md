Key decisions (all pinned by prompt17, the reviews, or workspace precedent):

- **D1: `pg_locks` cannot prove pre-BEGIN admission (proven live on PG 16.15).** Granted session-level and transaction-level advisory locks produce byte-identical `pg_locks` rows (`transactionid` NULL, `waitstart` NULL — the latter populates only for waiting locks). PostgreSQL 16 exposes neither session-vs-xact advisory ownership nor acquisition order relative to the MVCC snapshot. Execution reproduces the stale-RR attack red, then proves one of: (i) an XID admission ticket verified by visibility — `pg_visible_in_snapshot(ticket_xid, pg_current_snapshot())` plus direct visibility of the committed ticket record inside the caller's RR snapshot; top-level PostgreSQL-generated XID; ticket created by a separate READ COMMITTED operation committed before the RR capture transaction begins; the acquisition helper rejects invocation from an existing RR transaction; all 7 ticket cases tested (before/after/nested-issuance/foreign-backend/expired-or-released/old-xmin-pressure/aborted-txn); `xmin > ticket_xid` never the sole acceptance condition — or (ii) a restricted snapshot-execution boundary ordinary `app` cannot invoke (requires the privilege-change approval stop). Timestamps, session variables, and re-acquisition tricks are excluded by prompt17 §2.1 and advisory-lock reentrancy. Planning-time probes confirmed the visibility semantics both ways under real global-`xmin` pressure; execution re-proves inside the ticket lifecycle.
- **Lock substitution.** A held-lock check alone cannot prove the admission lock was never released and re-acquired as a transaction-level lock. The 6-step lock-swap attack is a committed failing test and must fail post-fix. Revoke direct `EXECUTE` on all 21 advisory-lock/unlock overloads from `PUBLIC`/`app` (revoke is per-database: provisioning plus a migration-time guard), with narrowly scoped SECURITY DEFINER wrappers as the only path; every legitimate lock operation uses the same canonical wrapper and key derivation. `app` must be unable to release an admission lock, substitute a transaction-level lock, acquire an unapproved lock identity, or bypass the ticket lifecycle. If unenforceable under two roles: stop at Gate 1, no silent third role.
